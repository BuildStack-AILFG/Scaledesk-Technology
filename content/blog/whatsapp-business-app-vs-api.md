Many businesses discover WhatsApp through the free WhatsApp Business app, then hit its limits and hear about "the API". They sound similar, but they are built for different stages of a business. Choosing the wrong one costs time, so it is worth understanding the difference before you commit.

## The WhatsApp Business app

This is the free app you install on a phone. It adds business features to normal WhatsApp: a business profile, quick replies, labels, a simple catalogue and basic away and greeting messages.

**Good for:** a very small business, one or two people replying, a few dozen chats a day, no need for automation or reporting.

**Limits you will notice:**

- It is tied to a phone, and only a small number of linked devices can use it at once.
- Automation is limited to simple greeting and away messages.
- There is no proper shared inbox, assignment, or reporting.
- Sending to large groups of customers is manual and restricted.

## The WhatsApp Business Platform (API)

The Business Platform is not an app you download. You connect your business number to it through software provided by a platform, and your team works inside that software instead.

**Good for:** teams of several people, higher volumes, automation, integrations with a CRM, campaigns to opted-in customers and detailed reporting.

**What it adds:**

- A shared inbox where many agents work at once.
- Chat assignment, notes and conversation history.
- Automated flows and replies.
- Approved message templates for business-started messages.
- Connections to your CRM and other systems.

**What it involves:**

- Your number is registered to the platform, so it cannot be used in the normal app at the same time.
- Business-started messages use approved templates, and Meta charges for conversations under its own pricing, which changes over time. Check the current pricing before you plan volumes.

## Side by side

| | Business app | Business Platform (API) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to start | Free | Software fee plus Meta message charges |
| Team use | Very limited | Many agents on one inbox |
| Automation | Greeting and away messages | Flows, replies, notifications |
| Campaigns | Manual | Template-based, to opted-in customers |
| CRM link | No | Yes |
| Best for | Solo or tiny teams | Growing teams and higher volumes |

## How to decide

Ask three questions:

1. **How many people reply?** More than two, and the app starts to hurt.
2. **How many chats do you handle each day?** If you are missing messages, you have outgrown it.
3. **Do you want automation or a CRM link?** If yes, you need the Platform.

If you are unsure, start with the app, keep your chat volume and response times in view, and move when the friction shows up. Moving later means re-registering your number, so plan the switch for a quiet period.

## Moving from the app to the Platform

1. Decide which platform you will use and confirm it supports the features you need.
2. Back up important chats, because history does not automatically move across.
3. Register your number with the platform and complete the business verification.
4. Prepare your first templates and flows before you switch, so customers see a smooth change.
5. Tell your team how the new inbox works.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use the same number on the app and the API?

Generally not at the same time. Once a number is registered on the Business Platform, it is used through your platform software instead of the phone app. Some setups allow limited coexistence, so check current Meta documentation.

### Is the API free?

The Platform itself is accessed through a provider that charges a software fee, and Meta charges for business conversations. Pricing changes, so confirm the current details before you decide.

### Will I lose my chat history when I switch?

History from the phone app is not automatically carried over. Export anything important first.
