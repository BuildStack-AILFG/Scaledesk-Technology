Spreadsheets are a fine place to start. They are flexible, familiar and free. But as leads and team members grow, they begin to fail: nobody knows the latest version, follow-ups get missed, and conversations live somewhere else entirely. Here is how to move to a CRM without chaos.

## Why spreadsheets stop working

- **No history.** You see a name and a status, not the conversation.
- **No reminders.** Nothing tells you a follow-up is due.
- **Version confusion.** Several copies, several truths.
- **Weak collaboration.** Two people cannot safely edit at the same time or see who owns what.
- **No connection to channels.** Messages and emails are separate.

## Step 1: Audit what you have

List every place customer information lives: spreadsheets, phone contacts, email, WhatsApp chats and notebooks. Decide which are the sources of truth and which are just copies.

## Step 2: Decide what to keep

Not every old row deserves a place in the new system. Consider:

- **Active leads and customers:** migrate.
- **Recent inactive contacts:** migrate and mark them.
- **Very old or bad data:** archive rather than import.

## Step 3: Clean before you import

Importing a mess just gives you a mess in a new place.

- Remove duplicates.
- Standardise phone numbers and names.
- Fix obvious errors.
- Fill in missing owners and sources.

Use our [CRM data hygiene checklist](/blog/crm-data-hygiene-checklist) as a guide.

## Step 4: Map your fields

Match each spreadsheet column to a CRM field. Typical columns: name, phone, email, company, source, status, owner, notes, last contacted. Decide where extra columns should go, or whether they are worth keeping at all.

## Step 5: Set up your pipeline and rules

Before importing, configure your stages, sources and owners in the CRM. Our guide to [pipeline stages](/blog/crm-pipeline-stages-guide) helps you design them. Convert the status column in your spreadsheet into these stages.

## Step 6: Test with a small batch

Import twenty or thirty rows first. Check that names, numbers and statuses landed in the right fields. Fix the mapping and only then import the rest.

## Step 7: Train the team

- Show people what changes in their day, not every feature.
- Agree three simple rules, such as "every lead has an owner" and "every lead has a next step".
- Let people ask questions during the first week.

## Step 8: Set a switch-over date

Choose a date after which the spreadsheet becomes read-only. Running both systems for long causes confusion. Keep the old file as a backup, but everyone works in the CRM.

## Step 9: Connect your channels

The real gain comes when conversations enter the CRM automatically: forms, WhatsApp, Instagram and email. Otherwise people fall back on copying details by hand.

## Step 10: Review after 30 days

Ask the team what is working and what is annoying. Adjust fields and stages. Check that leads are being followed up faster.

## Common problems and fixes

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| The team keeps using the spreadsheet | Make it read-only on the switch-over date |
| Data is missing | Make key fields required |
| People forget to update | Add reminders and automate stage changes |
| Nobody owns the system | Appoint a CRM owner |

## A sensible timeline

For a small team, this can take days rather than months: one for the audit and clean-up, one for setup and import, one for training, and then a week of close attention.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I import a spreadsheet into a CRM?

Most CRMs accept CSV imports. Save your spreadsheet as a CSV, map the columns and import.

### What if my team resists the change?

Focus on how it saves them time, keep the rules few and involve them in setting up the pipeline.

### Should I keep the spreadsheet as a backup?

Yes, keep a read-only copy for reference, but make the CRM the single working place.
