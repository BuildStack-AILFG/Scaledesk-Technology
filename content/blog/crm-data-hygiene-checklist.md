A CRM is only as good as the data inside it. When records are duplicated, out of date or half-filled, people stop trusting the system and go back to spreadsheets. Regular data hygiene keeps your CRM useful and your reports honest.

## Signs your data needs attention

- The same customer appears two or three times.
- Phone numbers are in different formats.
- Nobody is sure who owns a lead.
- Reports show numbers that colleagues do not believe.
- Leads from months ago are still marked as "new".

## The checklist

### 1. Remove duplicates

Duplicates cause double messages, confusion and wrong totals.

- Search by phone number and email, which are the most reliable identifiers.
- Merge duplicates, keeping the most complete record and the full conversation history.
- Prevent new duplicates by checking for an existing record when someone is added.

### 2. Standardise formats

- Store phone numbers in one format, ideally with the country code.
- Use consistent capitalisation for names and companies.
- Use dropdown lists instead of free text for fields like source, status and industry.

### 3. Fill the essential fields

Decide the minimum every lead must have: name, phone or email, source, owner and status. Make those fields required where possible. Do not require twenty fields, or people will type junk to get past the form.

### 4. Make ownership clear

Every open lead needs one owner and a next step. Review leads with no owner or no scheduled task every week.

### 5. Close out stale records

Leads that have not moved for a long time inflate your pipeline.

- Follow up once more.
- If there is no response, mark them lost or move them to a nurture list.
- Record a reason so you learn from it.

### 6. Track the source properly

Knowing where leads come from tells you where to spend money. Use a fixed list of sources such as "Instagram DM", "Website form" and "Referral", and avoid free-text variations.

### 7. Respect consent and privacy

Record how and when a person agreed to be contacted, especially for messaging channels. Remove data when people ask, and limit access to those who need it. Our guide on [opt-in best practices](/blog/whatsapp-opt-in-best-practices) explains why consent records matter.

### 8. Back up and export

Make sure you can export your data regularly. It protects you from mistakes and prevents lock-in.

## Build hygiene into habits

- **Daily:** update leads after each conversation.
- **Weekly:** review leads with no next step.
- **Monthly:** check duplicates and stale deals.
- **Quarterly:** review fields and dropdowns, and remove unused ones.

## Who should own data quality?

Choose one person as the CRM owner. They do not fix everything, but they set the rules, check quality and answer questions. Without an owner, hygiene slowly disappears.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I clean my CRM?

Do light checks weekly and a fuller review monthly. Small regular efforts beat a large annual clean-up.

### What is the most common data problem?

Duplicates and missing ownership. Fix these two first.

### Should I delete old leads?

Usually archive rather than delete, unless the person asks for removal or the law requires it.
