Customer support is a natural place for AI agents, because so many questions repeat: order status, opening hours, returns, how to use a feature. Answering them instantly at any hour improves service and frees your team for problems that need thought. Done poorly, though, it traps customers in loops. This guide shows how to do it well.

## The kinds of questions an agent handles well

- **Information.** Hours, locations, prices, policies.
- **Status checks.** "Where is my order?" when connected to the right system.
- **How-to help.** Step-by-step answers from your help articles.
- **Simple requests.** Rescheduling, updating contact details or requesting a callback.
- **Triage.** Gathering details so a person can solve the problem faster.

## The kinds it should hand over

- Complaints and upset customers.
- Refunds, disputes and exceptions.
- Complex technical problems.
- Anything involving safety, legal or financial risk.
- Requests that the customer wants a person for.

## Step 1: Study your existing questions

Look at the last few hundred messages or tickets. Group them by topic. Usually a small number of topics account for most volume. Those are your first candidates.

## Step 2: Write the source material

An agent is only as accurate as the information it draws from. Prepare:

- A clear, current FAQ.
- Policies written in plain language.
- Step-by-step guides.
- Examples of good replies in your voice.

Keep them updated. Old prices and policies produce wrong answers.

## Step 3: Set the boundaries

Decide what the agent may do and say. For example, it may explain the returns policy but not approve exceptions. Our article on [AI agent guardrails](/blog/ai-agent-guardrails-human-in-the-loop) shows how.

## Step 4: Design the hand-off

The most important moment is when the agent cannot help. It should:

- Say so honestly.
- Offer a person or a call-back.
- Pass a summary so the customer does not have to repeat themselves.

Read about this in [chatbot hand-off design](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-with-human-handoff).

## Step 5: Work on the channels customers use

Support conversations happen on WhatsApp, Instagram, email and the phone. A shared inbox that brings them together, with the agent answering first and people stepping in, keeps the experience consistent. See [TalkForGrow](/products/talkforgrow) and [EngageForGrow](/products/engageforgrow) for messaging platforms.

## Step 6: Measure

- First response time.
- Share of questions resolved without a person.
- Escalation rate and whether escalations were appropriate.
- Customer satisfaction.
- Repeat contacts on the same issue.

## Step 7: Improve constantly

Each week, read a sample of conversations. Where did the agent fail? Add or fix an answer. Where did customers escalate? Improve the hand-off or expand the scope carefully.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Hiding the human option.** It frustrates customers.
- **Letting it guess.** It should say when it does not know.
- **Launching everywhere at once.** Start with a few topics.
- **Ignoring feedback.** Customers will tell you what is wrong if you ask.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an AI agent handle all my support?

No, and it should not try. Aim for routine questions first and keep people for complex or sensitive cases.

### How long does it take to set up?

A focused first version, covering your most common questions, can be prepared quickly. Improvement then continues weekly.

### Will customers accept talking to AI?

Most accept it when it is fast, honest and easy to escape to a person. They dislike it when it wastes their time.
