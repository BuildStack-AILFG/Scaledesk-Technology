CRM automation means letting your CRM do the repetitive steps in your sales process so your team can spend time on conversations. It does not have to be complicated. A handful of well-chosen workflows removes most of the manual work and, more importantly, stops leads from slipping through the cracks.

## How a workflow is built

Every workflow has three parts:

1. **A trigger.** Something happens, such as a new lead arriving or a deal moving stage.
2. **Conditions.** Optional checks, such as "the lead came from Instagram" or "the deal value is above a set amount".
3. **Actions.** What the CRM does: assign an owner, send a message, create a task, update a field.

If you can describe a process as "when X happens, do Y", you can automate it.

## 1. Route new leads to the right person

**Trigger:** a new lead is created.
**Actions:** assign the lead to a salesperson by territory, product interest or round-robin, and notify them.

This removes the delay of someone manually deciding who takes each enquiry. Speed of first response often decides who wins the deal.

## 2. Send an instant acknowledgement

**Trigger:** a new lead arrives from a form, message or ad.
**Actions:** send a short thank-you that says when someone will be in touch.

The customer knows you received their enquiry, and your team gets breathing room. If you use WhatsApp for this, read about [approved templates](/blog/whatsapp-message-templates-guide) first.

## 3. Create a follow-up task automatically

**Trigger:** a lead is created, or a call is logged with no next step.
**Actions:** create a task for the owner due in one or two days.

Every lead should always have a next step with a date. This workflow guarantees it.

## 4. Nudge leads that go quiet

**Trigger:** no activity on a lead for a set number of days.
**Actions:** send a gentle follow-up or remind the owner to call.

Keep it helpful rather than pushy. A sequence of two or three well-spaced messages beats daily pestering. See an example in our [7-day WhatsApp follow-up sequence](/blog/whatsapp-lead-follow-up-sequence).

## 5. Update the stage when something happens

**Trigger:** a quote is sent, a meeting is booked, a payment is received.
**Actions:** move the deal to the matching pipeline stage.

Your pipeline stays accurate without people remembering to update it. Learn how to design the stages in [CRM pipeline stages explained](/blog/crm-pipeline-stages-guide).

## 6. Flag hot leads

**Trigger:** a lead reaches a score threshold, replies quickly, or visits your pricing page.
**Actions:** notify the owner and raise the priority.

This pairs well with [lead scoring](/blog/lead-scoring-basics-for-small-teams), which tells you which leads deserve attention first.

## 7. Hand off from sales to onboarding

**Trigger:** a deal is marked won.
**Actions:** create onboarding tasks, notify the delivery team and send a welcome message.

A smooth start builds trust and reduces the risk of a customer feeling forgotten after paying.

## 8. Recover lost deals later

**Trigger:** a deal is marked lost, with a reason.
**Actions:** schedule a check-in for a few months later.

Circumstances change. A short, respectful note months later brings back a meaningful share of customers who were not ready the first time.

## Rules for good automation

- **Start with one or two workflows** and check they work before adding more.
- **Write messages as if a person sent them.**
- **Give people a way to opt out** and honour it.
- **Keep a person in the loop** for anything sensitive or high-value.
- **Review monthly.** Turn off workflows that no longer help.
- **Avoid over-messaging.** Set limits on how often one person can be contacted.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will automation make my business feel impersonal?

Not if you use it for routine steps and keep people involved in real conversations. Customers notice slow replies more than automated ones.

### How many workflows do I need?

Most small teams do well with three to five. More is not better; useful is better.

### Do I need a developer to set up CRM workflows?

Usually not. Most modern CRMs let you set up simple workflows with a visual builder. Complex integrations may need technical help.
