Followers are not customers. A follower is someone who paid a small amount of attention to you once. The businesses that make money from Instagram build a simple path from that attention to a conversation, and from a conversation to a sale. This guide walks through that path.

## Step 1: Make your profile a clear starting point

Your profile is your shop window. Within a few seconds a visitor should understand what you do and what to do next.

- **Say what you offer and who it is for** in the bio.
- **Add one clear action**, such as "Message us to book" or a link to a booking page.
- **Turn on the message button** and make sure your contact details are correct.
- **Highlight the essentials** such as pricing, delivery and reviews in story highlights.

## Step 2: Create content that invites a conversation

Content that only shows off products earns likes. Content that asks or answers questions earns messages.

- Post answers to the questions customers ask most.
- Show the process behind your work.
- Invite a specific reply: "Message us your city and we will tell you the delivery time."
- Use stories with polls and question boxes, and follow up personally on answers.

## Step 3: Reply fast

Speed decides a lot. A person who asks about price is comparing options, and the business that replies first often wins. Set a target response time, for example under five minutes during working hours, and use an automated first reply to cover the rest. ## Step 4: Qualify with two or three questions

Do not interrogate people, but a couple of simple questions help you understand who you are talking to:

- What are you looking for?
- When do you need it?
- Which area or budget range is right for you?

These answers tell you whether to send a quote, share a brochure or hand the chat to a specialist.

## Step 5: Capture the lead properly

The moment a person shares a phone number, email or clear intent, record them as a lead. Note:

- Their name and contact details.
- The source ("Instagram DM, reel about product X").
- What they asked for.
- The next step and who owns it.

## Step 6: Follow up

Most leads need more than one touch. Send a helpful follow-up the next day, then a lighter reminder a few days later. Keep each message useful, and stop when they ask you to. ## Step 7: Learn which content brings customers

Track which posts, reels and stories lead to conversations and sales, not just to likes. Label the source on every lead, and review monthly. Double down on what works and drop what does not.

## A simple weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** review last week's leads and outcomes.
- **Daily:** answer messages and comments; check hand-offs.
- **Twice a week:** publish content that invites questions.
- **Friday:** update your CRM and plan next week's follow-ups.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many followers do I need to get leads?

Far fewer than you think. A small, relevant audience that trusts you often produces more leads than a large one that does not.

### Should I put prices on Instagram?

It depends on your business. If price is the first question, sharing a range saves time for both sides.

### How quickly should I reply?

As fast as you reasonably can. Minutes matter for buying enquiries, so use an automated first reply and route serious enquiries to a person.
