"AI agent" is one of the most overused phrases in technology, so it helps to define it simply. An AI agent is software that can understand a goal, decide what steps to take, and carry them out using your business tools, while staying within rules you set. It is different from a tool that only answers questions.

## Agents, chatbots and automation rules

These three are often confused.

| | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Automation rule** | Follows a fixed "if this, then that" path | When a form is submitted, send a confirmation |
| **Chatbot** | Answers questions, often from a script or a knowledge base | Replies to "what are your opening hours?" |
| **AI agent** | Works toward a goal, using judgement and tools | Qualifies a lead, books a meeting, updates the CRM |

The lines blur, and most useful systems combine all three. What matters is whether the tool reliably does a useful job within limits you control.

## What agents can do for a business today

- **Voice.** Answer and make phone calls, take messages and book appointments.
- **Sales.** Reply to new leads within minutes, ask qualifying questions and hand promising ones to a person.
- **Support.** Resolve common customer questions at any hour and escalate difficult ones.
- **Finance.** Send invoices, chase overdue payments politely and help reconcile records.
- **Hiring.** Screen applications, schedule interviews and answer candidates' questions.
- **Marketing.** Draft campaigns, segment audiences and summarise results.

Each of these is a set of repetitive, rules-based tasks that people find tiring, and where speed and consistency matter.

## What agents should not do alone

Agents are not a replacement for judgement. Keep a person in charge of:

- Decisions with legal, financial or safety consequences.
- Sensitive conversations, such as complaints and bereavement.
- Exceptions to policy.
- Anything the agent is uncertain about.

## How to decide where to use an agent

Good candidates share four traits:

1. **High volume.** The task happens many times a week.
2. **Clear rules.** You can explain what "good" looks like.
3. **Low risk per mistake.** Errors are easy to spot and fix.
4. **Real cost of delay.** Speed improves the outcome, as with new leads.

Start with one task, measure it, then expand.

## What you need first

Agents are only as good as what they can access. Prepare:

- **Accurate information.** Prices, policies, opening hours and answers to common questions.
- **Connected tools.** A CRM, calendar or messaging channel, so the agent can act, not just talk.
- **Clear boundaries.** What the agent can say, what it cannot promise, and when to escalate.
- **Someone accountable.** A person who reviews conversations and improves the setup.

## How to measure success

- Response time before and after.
- Share of tasks completed without a person.
- Escalations and whether they were appropriate.
- Customer feedback.
- Time saved for your team.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will AI agents replace my team?

They take over repetitive tasks so people can focus on work that needs judgement and relationships. The best results come from agents and people working together.

### Are AI agents safe to use with customers?

They can be, with clear boundaries, tested responses, easy escalation to a person and regular review of conversations.

### Do I need technical staff to use an AI agent?

Not necessarily. You do need someone who understands your processes and can review results.
