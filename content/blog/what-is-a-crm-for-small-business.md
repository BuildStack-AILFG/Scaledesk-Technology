CRM stands for customer relationship management. In practice, a CRM is a shared place where your business keeps track of every person and company you sell to, every conversation you have had with them, and what should happen next.

## What a CRM actually does

Think of everything you currently keep in your head, in notebooks, in phone contacts, in email folders and in chat apps. A CRM gathers it into one system:

- **Contacts and companies.** Who they are and how to reach them.
- **Leads and deals.** Who is interested, what they want and how far along they are.
- **Conversations.** Calls, emails and chats, in one timeline.
- **Tasks and reminders.** The next step for each person, with a date.
- **Reports.** How many leads you have, where they come from and what closes.

## A day in the life with a CRM

A customer messages your business asking for a quote. In the CRM, a lead is created with the source and the message. It is assigned to a salesperson, who sees a task to reply today. They send a quote, and the deal moves to the "Quote sent" stage. Three days later the CRM reminds them to follow up. When the customer agrees, the deal is marked won and delivery is notified.

Nothing was forgotten and nobody had to ask "who is handling this?".

## Signs you need a CRM

- Leads are stored in several places: spreadsheets, WhatsApp, email and someone's notebook.
- Customers get asked the same questions twice because colleagues cannot see past conversations.
- Follow-ups depend on memory.
- You cannot say how many leads you got last month or which source produced the best ones.
- When someone leaves, their customer knowledge leaves with them.

If two or three of these sound familiar, a CRM will likely pay for itself. We cover the move in [moving from spreadsheets to a CRM](/blog/move-from-spreadsheets-to-crm).

## Features that matter for small teams

1. **Easy lead capture** from forms, messages and ads.
2. **A visual pipeline** showing deals by stage.
3. **Tasks and reminders** so every lead has a next step.
4. **A shared inbox** for the channels your customers use, such as WhatsApp, Instagram and email.
5. **Simple automation** for routing, acknowledgements and follow-ups.
6. **Clear reports** that answer your real questions.
7. **Ease of use.** If your team finds it painful, they will not use it.

Features you can usually ignore at first: complex forecasting, elaborate custom objects and enterprise-grade permissions.

## Common misconceptions

- **"A CRM is only for big companies."** Small teams benefit most, because each lost lead matters.
- **"It will replace the human touch."** The opposite: it frees time for real conversations.
- **"It takes months to set up."** A simple setup takes days. Start small and expand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a CRM the same as a database of contacts?

No. A contact list stores details. A CRM also tracks conversations, deals, tasks and outcomes, and helps you decide what to do next.

### How long does it take to see benefits?

Many teams notice fewer missed leads within the first few weeks, because follow-ups are visible and assigned.

### What does a CRM cost?

Prices vary widely, from free entry plans to premium enterprise tools. Choose based on the features you will actually use.
