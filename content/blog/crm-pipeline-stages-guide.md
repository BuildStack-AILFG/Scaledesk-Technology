A sales pipeline is the set of stages a deal passes through from first contact to closing. Good stages make it obvious where each deal stands and what to do next. Poorly designed ones confuse the team and make reports meaningless.

## What a stage should represent

A stage is not a mood or a hunch. It is a clear, observable milestone in the buyer's journey, ideally something the customer did or agreed to. For example, "Quote sent" is a fact. "Feeling positive" is not.

## A simple starting pipeline

For many small businesses, five to seven stages are enough.

| Stage | What it means | Typical next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New | Enquiry received, nobody has replied | Reply within the target time |
| Contacted | You have made first contact | Qualify their needs |
| Qualified | Need, budget and timing are confirmed | Prepare a quote or demo |
| Proposal sent | The customer has a quote or offer | Follow up in two days |
| Negotiation | Details or price being discussed | Agree terms |
| Won | The customer has agreed or paid | Start delivery |
| Lost | The deal will not go ahead | Record the reason |

## Define entry criteria for each stage

For every stage, write one sentence stating what must be true for a deal to enter it. For example, "Qualified" means you know the customer's need, their rough budget and when they want to buy. Without this, two salespeople will put similar deals in different stages and your reports will not compare.

## Tips for designing stages

1. **Base them on the customer's journey,** not on your internal paperwork.
2. **Keep them few.** Too many stages create busywork; too few hide problems.
3. **Make the order clear.** A deal should move forward, not sideways.
4. **Always have a next step.** Every open deal needs a task with a date.
5. **Record why deals are lost.** Reasons like price, timing or competitor show you what to improve.
6. **Set a time limit per stage.** A deal stuck for too long needs attention.

## Different businesses, different pipelines

- **A service business** may add "Site visit" and "Estimate".
- **An online seller** may need a much shorter pipeline focused on conversation and payment.
- **A B2B sales team** may include "Demo" and "Security review".

Copy the pattern, then adapt the names to your language.

## Use the pipeline to spot problems

Once the stages are working you can see:

- **Where deals pile up,** indicating a bottleneck.
- **Where deals are lost,** indicating a weak step.
- **How long deals take,** helping you forecast.
- **Which sources convert,** guiding marketing spend.

## Common mistakes

- Creating a stage for every tiny action.
- Leaving old deals open forever, which inflates the pipeline.
- Letting each salesperson define their own stages.
- Never reviewing the pipeline with the team.

Review the pipeline weekly: for each open deal, ask "what is the next step, and who owns it?" A tool like [LeadForGrow](/products/leadforgrow-crm) gives you a visual pipeline where each deal shows its stage, owner and next task.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many pipeline stages should I have?

Usually five to seven. Start simple and add a stage only when it clarifies something you cannot see today.

### Should I have more than one pipeline?

Yes, if you sell very different things with different processes, such as new sales and renewals.

### What should I do with stale deals?

Follow up once more, then close them as lost with a reason. Keeping dead deals open makes your numbers unreliable.
