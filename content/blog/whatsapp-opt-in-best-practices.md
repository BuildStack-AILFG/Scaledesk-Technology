WhatsApp is built around conversations people want to have. That is why it expects businesses to get permission, called opt-in, before sending messages they start. Getting this right protects your number, your quality rating and your reputation, and it improves results too: people who asked to hear from you actually read your messages.

## What opt-in means

Opt-in is a clear signal from a person that they agree to receive messages from your business on WhatsApp. It should be:

- **Specific.** They know it is WhatsApp and roughly what you will send.
- **Voluntary.** Not hidden in a long block of terms or pre-ticked without their notice.
- **Recorded.** You can show when and how they agreed.

WhatsApp's own policies set the requirements, and they can change, so read the current business policy. Local privacy law also applies, and it differs by country, so take advice for your market.

## Good places to collect opt-in

- **Your website forms.** Add a separate tick box such as "Send me updates on WhatsApp", unticked by default.
- **Checkout or booking flows.** Ask at the moment the customer gives their number, and explain what you will send, for example order updates.
- **In-store or event sign-ups.** Use a QR code that opens a chat, so the customer starts the conversation themselves.
- **Click-to-WhatsApp links and ads.** When a person taps to message you, they have started the conversation, which opens the customer service window.
- **Existing conversations.** If a customer messages you first, you can ask if they would like updates, and record their yes.

## What to avoid

- **Buying or scraping phone numbers.** These people never agreed and will report you.
- **Assuming a phone number in your CRM means consent.** Having a number is not permission.
- **Bundling consent** into terms and conditions with no clear WhatsApp choice.
- **Importing an old list** and blasting it. Cold lists generate blocks and reports.

## Say what you will send, and how often

Tell people what to expect: "We will send order updates and occasional offers, about twice a month." Setting expectations reduces opt-outs later.

## Make opting out easy

Every marketing message should give a clear way to stop, such as a reply keyword or a button. Honour it immediately, and mark the contact as opted out in your CRM so no other campaign reaches them.

## Keep a proper record

For each contact, store:

- The phone number.
- That they opted in to WhatsApp messages.
- The date and source (form, checkout, chat).
- The wording they agreed to.
- Any later opt-out.

## Opt-in and the 24-hour window

These are related but different. Opt-in gives you permission to send business-started template messages. The [24-hour window](/blog/whatsapp-24-hour-conversation-window) opens whenever a customer messages you and lets you reply freely. You need to respect both.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need opt-in if the customer messaged me first?

If they messaged you, you can reply within the customer service window. For business-started messages later, you still need their opt-in.

### Is a phone number from a business card opt-in?

Generally no. Handing over a number is not the same as agreeing to receive WhatsApp marketing. Ask clearly.

### What happens if I ignore opt-in rules?

You risk blocks and reports, a lower quality rating, reduced sending limits and, in serious cases, restrictions on your number.
