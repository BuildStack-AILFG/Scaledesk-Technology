There are hundreds of CRMs. Most comparison lists rank tools by feature counts, which is rarely the right way to choose. The best CRM for you is the one your team will actually use, that fits how you sell and connects to where your customers talk to you. This checklist walks you through the choice.

## Step 1: Describe your sales process first

Before looking at any product, write down how a customer moves from first contact to payment. For example: enquiry, first reply, qualification call, quote, negotiation, payment, delivery. If you cannot describe the process, no tool will fix it.

## Step 2: List your must-have features

Split needs into three groups.

- **Must have.** Without these the tool is useless to you, such as lead capture, a pipeline and tasks.
- **Should have.** They make life easier, such as automation and reports.
- **Nice to have.** Ignore these while comparing.

Beware of paying for advanced features you will not use for a year.

## Step 3: Check the channels

Where do your customers actually contact you? If it is WhatsApp, Instagram and email, choose a CRM that brings those conversations in. A CRM that only stores phone numbers leaves you copying and pasting between apps.

## Step 4: Consider your team

- **How many people** will use it, and what are their roles?
- **How comfortable** are they with software?
- **Do they work on phones?** Many field and sales teams live on mobile.
- **Who will administer it?** Someone needs to own setup and rules.

## Step 5: Look at integrations

List the tools the CRM must work with: accounting, forms, calendars, payments and messaging. Confirm each connection exists and how it works, rather than trusting a logo on a webpage.

## Step 6: Understand the true cost

Ask about:

- Price per user, and what counts as a user.
- Limits on contacts, messages or automation.
- Fees for extra channels or integrations.
- Setup, training and support costs.
- What happens to your data if you leave.

## Step 7: Run a real trial

Do not judge from a demo. Take two or three shortlisted tools and test them with real data for one or two weeks:

1. Import a sample of your contacts.
2. Create a few leads and move them through the pipeline.
3. Set up one automation.
4. Have two colleagues use it for a normal day.
5. Ask them what annoyed them.

## Step 8: Check support and security

- How do you reach support, and how fast do they reply?
- Where is data stored, and who can access it?
- Can you control permissions by role?
- Can you export all your data?

## Common buying mistakes

- **Choosing by brand or feature count** rather than fit.
- **Skipping the process step**, so the CRM mirrors a mess.
- **Not involving the team**, which leads to low adoption.
- **Over-customising at the start.**
- **Ignoring the channels** customers actually use.

## A simple scorecard

Score each shortlisted tool from 1 to 5 on: ease of use, channel coverage, automation, reporting, support and total cost. Add the scores, then discuss the top two with your team.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I choose the cheapest CRM?

Price matters, but adoption matters more. A cheap tool that nobody uses costs more than a fair-priced one that everyone does.

### Can I switch CRMs later?

Yes, if you can export your data. Ask about export formats before you commit.

### How many CRMs should I trial?

Two or three. More than that creates confusion without better decisions.
